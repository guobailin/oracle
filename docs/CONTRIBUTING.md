# Contributing

We welcome contributions that improve evidence quality, reproducibility, data provenance, and scholarly review.

## Useful contributions

- Bibliographic references and resource metadata.
- Rights-cleared datasets or links to authorized source material.
- Corrections to catalogue metadata with citations.
- Reproducible image-processing and retrieval code.
- Benchmark definitions and documented evaluation results.
- Individual case dossiers with competing hypotheses.
- Expert review and methodological critique.

## Rights and responsible data use

Do not upload:
- scans or photographs from restricted databases without permission;
- copyrighted book or article scans without a lawful basis;
- datasets whose license does not permit the proposed use;
- scraped or mirrored material where terms prohibit it.

A resource being viewable online does not mean it may be redistributed or used for model training. When in doubt, contribute metadata and a source link rather than the asset.

## Evidence requirements

Every substantive palaeographic or historical claim should:
- identify the artifact and catalogue locator precisely;
- cite the source, page/plate/entry, or stable URL;
- distinguish direct visual observation from reading and interpretation;
- list plausible alternatives and counter-evidence;
- identify uncertainty and missing evidence;
- state whether AI was used and, if so, the exact model and experiment metadata.

Do not submit generated citations without checking the cited source.

## Pull request checklist

- [ ] Scope and purpose are clear.
- [ ] Sources and rights metadata are included.
- [ ] No unauthorized third-party material is committed.
- [ ] Data follows the current schema.
- [ ] Code includes reproducible instructions and tests where appropriate.
- [ ] Model IDs, prompts, and evaluation setup are documented.
- [ ] Claims distinguish observation, reading, and interpretation.
- [ ] Known limitations and disagreements are disclosed.

## Review policy

Machine-generated suggestions are not accepted as scholarly conclusions without evidence and human review. High-impact readings should seek independent review. Reviewers should be able to disagree without having their alternative silently overwritten.

## Conduct

Be respectful, precise, and open about uncertainty. Critique arguments and evidence, not contributors.
